Rent or buy? 1,000 American futures

A U.S. version of the CBC News rent-or-buy simulator. Each run follows three households for up to 30 years — one buys with a 30-year fixed mortgage, one buys with an adjustable-rate mortgage, one rents and invests — through 1,000 futures rebuilt from 25 years (2001–2025) of real U.S. home prices, rents, inflation, interest rates and market returns. It shows probabilities, not predictions.

Each dot is one simulated future, colored by the household with the highest net worth after selling the home and paying taxes.

How the simulator works

Three households, one budget

All three start with the same cash: the down payment plus buyer closing costs. The renter invests that cash on day one. Every month, the simulator computes each household's housing outlay; whichever pays less invests the difference in a stock and bond mix. This mirrors the CBC design (savings from cheaper housing are invested), extended both ways: when rent overtakes an owner's payment, the owner invests the gap instead.

1,000 futures from 25 years of history

Each future is built by stringing together blocks of consecutive historical years (five by default) drawn at random from 2001–2025. A sampled year carries all of its values together — that metro's home price change, national rent growth, inflation, S&P 500 and 10-year Treasury returns, and the change in the 1-year Treasury yield — so relationships between them, like the 2008 crash coinciding with falling rates, are preserved. Like the CBC tool, the record covers about 25 years, so earlier housing cycles such as the early-1990s decline are not in the sample.

Mortgages

The fixed-rate owner locks today's rate for 30 years. The ARM owner pays the starting rate for the fixed period, then resets once a year to the index plus the margin, limited by the per-year and lifetime caps and floored at the margin. The index starts at today's 1-year Treasury yield and moves by the same year-to-year changes as the sampled history. Most new U.S. ARMs now reset every six months off SOFR; the 1-year Treasury is used because it has the full 25-year history. PMI is charged when the down payment is under 20% and stops when the balance reaches 78% of the original price, the automatic-termination point under the Homeowners Protection Act of 1998.

Taxes

When enabled, owners get the federal benefit of itemizing only to the extent mortgage interest (on up to $750,000 of debt) plus property and state taxes (under the SALT cap) exceed the standard deduction. The 2026 standard deduction is $32,200 joint or $16,100 single and grows with inflation. The SALT cap follows current law: $40,400 in 2026, rising 1% a year through 2029 and returning to $10,000 in 2030; the high-income phase-down is not modeled. Home sale gains up to $500,000 (joint) or $250,000 (single) are excluded under IRC §121, and investment gains are taxed at your capital-gains rate when everything is sold at the end. Dividend taxes during the holding period, state taxes on gains and depreciation recapture are not modeled.

What it leaves out

  • Case-Shiller tracks repeat sales of single-family homes. Condos can behave differently; Case-Shiller publishes condo indices for only a handful of metros.
  • Rent growth is the national CPI rent index, the only rent series with a long, consistent history. Local rents can run hotter or cooler, and CPI rent reflects all leases, not just new ones.
  • No refinancing, moving costs, renovations, special assessments, or the value of stability, rent control or the freedom to move.
  • Maintenance is a flat share of home value. Experts quoted by CBC noted real repair costs are often higher, especially when something big fails.
  • Insurance and property tax defaults are statewide averages. Florida, Texas and other storm-prone states often cost far more to insure; use a real quote.

Defaults that are assumptions, not data

Buyer closing costs (3%), PMI (0.5% of the loan), maintenance (1% of value), renter's insurance ($15/month), other selling costs (1%), the ARM starting rate (0.5 points below the fixed rate), margin (2.75), caps (2 per year, 5 lifetime), fund fees (0.1%), and the 60/40 stock-bond split are round-number starting points. Replace them with your own figures.

Data sources

  1. Home prices: S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Indices, 20 metro indices and U.S. National, not seasonally adjusted, December-to-December changes 2001–2025. Via FRED (mirrored by DataHub core/house-prices-us).
  2. Home prices for Lincoln and Omaha (not covered by Case-Shiller): FHFA All-Transactions House Price Index, Q4-to-Q4 changes, Lincoln, NE MSA and Omaha–Council Bluffs, NE-IA MSA. This index includes appraisals from refinances, so it moves more smoothly than Case-Shiller. Current values and rents from Zillow city pages for Lincoln and Omaha.
  3. Rent growth: BLS CPI-U, Rent of primary residence, U.S. city average (CUUS0000SEHA), semiannual averages combined into annual averages. FRED.
  4. Inflation: BLS CPI-U all items, not seasonally adjusted, December-to-December. Via datasets/cpi-us.
  5. Stock and bond returns: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern, Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills — S&P 500 with dividends and 10-year U.S. Treasury bond total returns, 2001–2025.
  6. ARM index history: Federal Reserve H.15, 1-year Treasury constant maturity, annual averages (FRED RIFLGFCY01NA); current level from multpl.com (Aug 2026).
  7. Today's mortgage rate: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, 30-year fixed 7.28% for the week of October 1, 2026.
  8. Home values and rents by metro: Zillow August 2026 Market Report (ZHVI and ZORI).
  9. Property tax: effective rates on owner-occupied housing from the 2024 American Community Survey (aggregate real estate taxes ÷ aggregate home value, tables B25090 and B25082), as compiled by The Florida Probate & Family Law Firm; U.S. average from NAHB. California is set to the Prop 13 base rate of 1% because statewide averages reflect older assessments.
  10. Home insurance: Freddie Mac research — average premium of $4.90 per $1,000 of home value in 2023, and under $2.50 per $1,000 in CA, WA, NV, OR and DC.
  11. Agent commissions: 5.44% average total, Clever Real Estate 2025 survey, reported by HousingWire.
  12. Tax parameters: 2026 standard deduction and SALT cap as summarized by Kiplinger; mortgage interest limit of $750,000 and §121 exclusion per IRS rules.
  13. Method: Nael Shiab, CBC News, "Is it better to buy or rent?", October 8, 2026.
